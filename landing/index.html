Transaction → plain words

Before you sign, Clara decodes the calldata, runs it past a deterministic policy engine, and tells you what it actually does — unlimited approvals, collection-wide NFT handovers, Permit2 batch drains, blind signatures, account delegation. The patterns that empty wallets.

Plain words → transaction

“Send 0.001 ETH to alice” becomes a real transaction — parsed by a local model, validated, built by the wallet kit, then checked back through the explainer before you confirm. The two directions verify each other.

the design inversion

The policy engine decides. The AI only narrates.

Clara never asks a language model “is this safe?” — that's where small models hallucinate. A deterministic rule engine produces the verdict; the local model only words it. Clara structurally cannot hallucinate “looks safe.”

decode

Calldata, typed data, and signatures decoded to operations. Attacks hidden in encodings are seen for what they do, not how they're written.

viem · deterministic
policy verdict

Spending caps, session limits, drain-pattern rules — ALLOW or DENY, with the rule that fired. Evaluated by the wallet kit's own policy engine.

WDK policy engine · deterministic
narrate

A 1.7B local model words the verdict in plain language. A guard rejects any narration that contradicts it; a template takes over if needed.

QVAC · on-device LLM
local means local

No cloud. Not “private cloud.” None.

Inference runs on your machine via QVAC. Keys live in a separate local process via WDK — outside the browser sandbox, where drainers can't reach them. The two talk over loopback: traffic that never touches the network card.
